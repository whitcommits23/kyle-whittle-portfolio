About

VoC infrastructure at Qualtrics, then a decade on the action side. Here's how I got here.

My Story

My background is a little different from most CX practitioners. Most people start in support and grow into the intelligence side. I came up the opposite direction.

I studied Industrial/Organizational Psychology at Brigham Young University-Idaho. That gave me a foundation in how people learn, perform, and change, plus a real appreciation for the data behind it. I joined Qualtrics in 2014, early, when the VoC category was still being defined. I came up inside the CX infrastructure before most organizations even had a formal CX function. I learned how signals get collected, how programs get built, and what makes customer data trustworthy before I ever had to act on it.

Then I spent the next decade on the action side. The teams whose job is to take an insight and actually do something with it: content, enablement, onboarding. The teams that close the loop.

At Qualtrics I built customer education that generated $1.6M in revenue and digitized 70+ courses on Skilljar. While I was doing that, I finished a Master of Education in Instructional Design at the University of Utah. Those years taught me how to design for scale: build the training, but also build the operational infrastructure around it.

I took a detour to McMaster-Carr in Cleveland and ran a customer contact center, then warehouse operations. Managing 10,000+ daily orders with a 25-person team turned me into a systems thinker the hard way. SOPs, throughput, capacity planning, the compounding return of process discipline. Operations is the cure for theater.

I came back to SaaS at Canopy and built the customer education function from scratch: Knowledge Base, LMS, video library, two certification programs. I led customer support, then customer onboarding and education globally. Cut average onboarding time from 3-4 months to 30-60 days. Contributed to a 40% churn drop in the first year after redesign. Finished an MBA at Quantic with a 4.0 along the way.

Today I lead Customer Knowledge and Content Strategy at Entrata. I run a Zendesk knowledge ecosystem at 3,500+ articles across 60+ product lines. I built a 4-agent AI pipeline that took standard article updates from 8 hours to 3, and complex articles from 14 hours to 90 minutes. AI is a force multiplier when you treat it like one. I use Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini daily.

What I took from both sides of CX: data alone doesn't move an organization. The teams receiving the insight have to feel equipped to act, and the win has to belong to them. That's shaped how I build.

Principles

What I Believe

Work must be results-driven

It's not enough to create something for the sake of creating. Every program, article, or system I build is tied to a measurable outcome: adoption, retention, efficiency, revenue. If I can't define what success looks like, I go back to the problem statement.

The learner is always the customer

Whether I'm building for internal teams or external customers, the person who has to learn the thing is the one I design for. That means understanding their prior knowledge, their context, and their constraints, then building something they'll actually use.

Professionalism builds trust

I've worked with adults across market research, healthcare, automotive, financial, and manufacturing industries. That breadth taught me to be flexible, direct, and to respect people's time. The work I deliver reflects that: clear, purposeful, and built to last.
